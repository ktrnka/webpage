# Urban detrashing tips

## Urban cleanups

### Your first litter pick up

Start small with an old grocery bag or a bucket. If you have gloves that's great. If not, old kitchen tongs work well.

### What to go for, and why

- **Plastics**: they break down into microplastics, enter waterways/food chains, and can choke pets. Picking up large plastics early prevents them being shredded into many small pieces by lawnmowers or cars.
- **Glass shards**: keep them out of shoes and away from pets; pack shards in a thick container or use other trash as a buffer so they don't tear bags.
- **Rusty metal**: to reduce tetanus risk.
- **Sharp metal** (nails, wire): in the road, to prevent popped tires. On the sidewalk, to keep people and pets safe.
- **Food containers / cups**: coatings often prevent biodegradation.
- **Glass bottles**: easier to clean up before they're broken.
- **Soda cans**: satisfying and easy to pick up. They also turn into a mess if a lawnmower runs over them.
- **Covid masks**: they break down slowly; pick them up when possible.
- **Garbage blocking drains**: can cause localized flooding.
- **Cigarette butts**: they leach chemicals; I rarely pick them all up because there are so many but I try to get some of them.

### Items I pick up less

- **Tissues / napkins**: break down quickly and add weight.
- **Cardboard**: usually degrades quickly.
- **Clothes**: heavy and often will break down.

### Rare items

- **Needles**: if found I keep them in a study container for disposal.
- Larger bulky items I generally leave unless in a group with dedicated pickup.

## Where the litter is / isn't

### High litter areas

- Schools
- Abandoned businesses
- Bus stops
- Construction areas

### Low litter areas

- Single-family residential away from arterials
- Active businesses

## Safety tips

- When cleaning roadsides, face traffic so you can see vehicles.
- For any items in the road, brush them towards the curb before picking them up; watch for parked cars that may move.
- Wear gloves and/or use grabbers/tongs.
- Stay off private property.
- If someone is camping/sleeping, don't disturb them.

## Special case: Seattle

- Seattle's Adopt-a-Street program supplies heavy trash bags, gloves, grabbers, high-visibility vests, and will collect bags left at city trash cans.
- Report large dumping sites with Seattle's "Find It, Fix It" app.

## Related: Graffiti / Stickers

Priorities: remove hate speech and anything affecting safety (e.g., obscured traffic signs) first; directional or cosmetic stickers are lower priority.

The items I've used most often:
- Glass scraper (the kind with a razor)
- Soap, water, scrub brush
- Isopropyl alcohol (IPA)
- Magic eraser / melamine sponge
- Rags

Tried and abandoned:
- Multi-purpose cleaner: Rarely more effective than soapy water
- Glass cleaner: Rarely more effective than soapy water
- Scraper / putty knife: This tends to scratch the underlying surface

New items being tried (2026):
- Graffiti safewipes: The adopt-a-street program gave me some. They were fantastic on the first try because they removed graffiti so quickly and easily compared to IPA. 

Approach:

- Start with the least-damaging method (soapy water, scrubbing) and escalate only if needed.
- Test in a small corner before aggressive cleaning; avoid damaging reflective surfaces on traffic signs.
- Matte paper stickers: They usually come apart with soapy water and a brush, then I remove the leftover residue with IPA
- Shiny stickers, thick paints/sealants: Use a glass scraper carefully (shallow angle, slicing motion, stabilize with both hands, watch for nearby people) and wear gloves.
